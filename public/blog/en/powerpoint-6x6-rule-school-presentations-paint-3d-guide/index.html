A+ PowerPoint & Google Slides Presentations (Grades 5–7): The 6×6 Rule, Contrast, Animations & 3D Models

Everyone has sat through a school presentation packed with tiny 12-point paragraphs pasted straight from an encyclopedia, unreadable yellow text, and dizzying spin animations on every bullet. In Grade 6 ICT, students learn visual communication rules that make slides crisp, memorable, and easy to follow—including embedding custom 3D assets from Paint 3D.

Quick Answer (TL;DR)

An A+ school deck follows the 6×6 Rule (no more than 6 bullets per slide, roughly 6 words per line), enforces large readable type (24 pt+ body, 36–44 pt titles), uses high text-to-background contrast, sticks to one subtle transition (Fade/Morph), and keeps spoken details inside Speaker Notes instead of crowding slides.

1. The 6×6 Rule & 24 pt Minimum: Why Fewer Words Win Higher Grades

A slide deck is NOT a printed essay! If the audience has to read 15 dense lines off the projector, they stop listening to you. Apply these five core ICT slide rules instead:

Slide Design Standards for PowerPoint & Google Slides
Design ElementCommon MistakePro Standard
Text DensityFull copied paragraphs pasted on slides.6×6 Rule: max 6 bullets per slide × ~6 words per line.
Font Size11–14 pt text unreadable from the back row.Titles 36–44 pt; body 24 pt minimum; max 2 font families.
Color ContrastLight green text on pale blue gradients.Dark charcoal text on crisp light slides—or pure white on deep navy.
Images & 3DStretched photos with stock watermarks.Proportional resizing (drag corners!) or clean transparent PNG/3D models.
TransitionsWild explosions + loud sound effects per slide.One calm transition (Fade or Morph) across the entire deck.
Transitions vs. Animations on ICT Quizzes

A Transition controls how you move BETWEEN two whole slides (Slide 1 -> Slide 2). An Animation controls how an individual object ON a slide enters, emphasizes, or exits.

2. A Foolproof 6-Slide Structure for Any School Topic

Whether presenting Renewable Energy, Ecosystems, or Cybersecurity, a tight 6-slide outline keeps timing crisp:

  1. Slide 1 — Cover: Short punchy title, subtitle, your name/grade, and one bold visual.
  2. Slide 2 — Roadmap: The 3–4 key questions your talk answers.
  3. Slides 3–4 — Core Concepts + Visuals: One main idea per slide backed by a SmartArt diagram, chart, or Paint 3D object.
  4. Slide 5 — Real-World Case Study: Concrete numbers or live demo showing why it matters.
  5. Slide 6 — Takeaways & Sources: Three closing bullets plus cited references.

3. Using Speaker Notes (Presenter View) & Custom 3D Objects

Where do detailed explanations go if slides stay short under the 6×6 rule? Inside the Speaker Notes pane beneath each slide! Launching Presenter View (Alt+F5) shows clean visuals on the classroom projector while displaying your notes and timer privately on your laptop.

Students can also export Paint 3D creations as .glb files or transparent PNGs and insert them via Insert -> 3D Models to rotate objects live while explaining them.

✅ Pre-Talk Slide Deck Checklist

  • Every slide follows the 6×6 rule with 24 pt+ readable body text.
  • High color contrast verified from across the room.
  • Images resized strictly from corner handles (never squished) and sources cited.
  • Backup PDF copy saved on USB/cloud in case the classroom PC lacks PowerPoint.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What is the 6×6 rule in PowerPoint presentations?
The 6×6 rule caps each slide at up to 6 bullet points with roughly 6 words per line so audiences grasp key ideas quickly and focus on the speaker.
What is the difference between slide transitions and animations?
Transitions animate the switch from one slide to the next, whereas animations move or highlight individual elements (titles, shapes, photos) within a single slide.
Why save a backup PDF export of a presentation?
Different computers or office suites can scramble custom fonts and layouts. A PDF backup guarantees your slides display reliably on any classroom computer.
How do you keep an image from looking stretched when resizing?
Always drag from a corner handle rather than the side or top edge handles so PowerPoint or Slides preserves the original aspect ratio.

Pixel, Resolution & RGB Color Model (0–255) →
A pixel (Picture Element) is the smallest colored square in a digital image or screen grid. In the RGB color model, each pixel blends Red, Green, and Blue light channels ranging from 0 to 255.

In audio/video editing, the Timeline arranges clips sequentially across multiple stacked tracks (Multitrack). FPS (Frames Per Second) measures how many images play each second (typically 30 or 60 FPS), while Keyframes mark timestamps where volume, zoom, or position changes smoothly.
